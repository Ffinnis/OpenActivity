Overview

CPU, memory, GPU, disk, network and energy on one screen, each with a live graph, plus where your memory and power are going.

What it does

Groups processes by app
Chrome’s renderers count toward Chrome, a shell and everything it runs toward Terminal, WebKit’s content processes toward Safari. Expand any app to see its processes.
Keeps 30 days of history
Every metric, per app, charted for the last 12 hours, 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, with the apps that used the most. Stored in one small file on your Mac.
Finds forgotten dev servers
Node, Python, Go, Ruby, Bun and more, grouped by project folder with the ports they hold. Idle ones are pointed out, and stopping them always asks first.
Tells you when an app misbehaves
A notification when an app keeps the CPU busy, keeps growing in memory, or writes or downloads heavily for minutes on end. You set the thresholds.
Lives in your menu bar
An icon, figures, small graphs or stacked figures, and a compact dashboard one click away. The icon turns into a warning sign when your Mac is under strain.
And the smaller things
Temperatures and fan speeds, the battery of your AirPods, mouse and keyboard, volume per app, and a share card of your Mac in light or dark.

Install

About two minutes. The build is ad-hoc signed and not notarized yet, which is why step three exists.

  1. Download

    Get OpenActivity.zip from the latest release.

  2. Move it to Applications

    Unzip it and drag OpenActivity into your Applications folder.

  3. Allow the first launch

    Open it, then choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security. Or run:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/OpenActivity.app

Prefer to build it yourself? Clone the repository, open OpenActivity.xcodeproj in Xcode 26 and press ⌘R.

What you run stays on your Mac.

No account, no analytics, no update checks. OpenActivity doesn’t make a single network request of its own, and its history is one SQLite file you can delete from Settings.
